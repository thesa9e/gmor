# Reading Key for the Reality Unfolding Diagram — Artificial Intelligence

**Status:** official authorial interpretation frame for generative models, working version<br>
**Version:** 0.1.1<br>
**Applies to:** [Reality Unfolding Diagram](../diagrams/reality-unfolding.en.svg)<br>
**Compatible with:** [`reality-unfolding-v0.1.0`](https://github.com/thesa9e/gmor/tree/reality-unfolding-v0.1.0)

## Purpose

This file is intended for a generative model asked to interpret the diagram. It defines how to read the diagram, but it does not prescribe a conclusion or response format and must not replace the source.

The goal is to reduce the chance that an artificial intelligence system completes missing relations from familiar philosophical, scientific, religious, fictional, or linguistic patterns and then presents that completion as content of the model.

## What the diagram itself specifies

The annotation defines:

- `(A ? B)` — relation cannot be defined: logic is absent.
- `(A | B)` — equilibrium: no transition occurs.
- `(A & B)` — connected pair before equilibrium.
- `(A, B)` — focus on `A`, balance is not actualized.
- `(A / B)` — focus on `A`, balance is actualized: transition.

Line types:

- actualization of focus;
- actualization of balance;
- logic exists, focus is undefined;
- logic exists, balance is undefined;
- logic is undefined;
- unfolding/folding of reality.

`A` and `B` are a pair of connected metaconcepts.

The rules below form the official authorial reading frame. They include the canonical premises required to preserve the diagram's generative order, but they do not replace the source diagram with a detailed interpretation.

## Operating rules

### 1. Read the model generatively, not merely descriptively

For each level under consideration, maintain an explicit ledger of already available:

- concepts;
- distinctions;
- relations;
- operations;
- scopes.

Do not use a downstream element as an intrinsic property of an upstream state.

Use “higher” and “lower” only for the order of actualizations. Each subsequent actualization unconditionally inherits the entire accumulated configuration of prior actualizations and adds the result of its own actualization.

Do not confuse this with the prohibition on backward attribution: what comes later must not be attributed to an earlier actualization, while everything actualized earlier is inherited throughout all subsequent actualizations.

### 2. Maintain an allowed-operations ledger

Before each inference, identify the operations required by that inference and verify that they are available at the current level.

If an operation is not yet defined, the inference is not merely false or unknown; it is not yet a well-formed internal move at that level.

### 3. Do not replace undefinability with uncertainty

`(A ? B)` denotes the inability to define the relation when logic is absent.

Do not convert it into a hidden variable whose value supposedly exists but is unknown to an observer.

### 4. Do not impose temporal order before time

Do not interpret node placement or reading order as temporal “before/after” while time is not yet available at the level being modeled.

Read the sequence as an order of actualizations: when a preceding actualization is in place, subsequent relations and operations become available or distinguishable. Do not turn this dependency into `if/then` branching or into a temporal sequence before time arises.

### 5. Type-check the question before answering it

For questions about early levels, identify the operations already embedded in the wording.

Pay particular attention to assumptions involving:

- time;
- causality;
- identity;
- distinction;
- observerhood;
- intention;
- space;
- possibility;
- probability;
- truth and falsity.

If the question requires an unavailable operation, state that first; only then, if useful, provide a retrospective external reading.

### 6. Do not attribute the semantics of downstream language to an upstream level

A label on an early node does not automatically imply that the early state itself contains the full semantics that a later observer associates with that label.

Keep separate:

- the node's label;
- the node's structural role;
- the ordinary semantics of the label in external language.

### 7. Do not resolve a paradox before checking operation scope

When a contradiction or recursion appears, first test whether downstream operations were imported into an upstream level.

Only after that should you evaluate whether the model itself is internally inconsistent.

### 8. Keep alternative interpretations separate

When the source supports several internally coherent readings, keep them separate rather than collapsing them for fluency.

For each interpretation, record:

- what in the source supports it;
- which additional assumption it introduces;
- what it excludes;
- where it stops being a minimal inference from the diagram.

### 9. Reconstruct inheritance as a cascade

At every transition, carry forward the entire accumulated configuration of prior actualizations, not only the result of the immediately preceding step. What is inherited includes metaconcepts, the relations and distinctions between them, defined limits, and available operations. What was defined earlier therefore persists throughout all subsequent actualizations.

In the sequence shown in the diagram, this includes, in particular, logic and the relation of necessity and limitation that it defines; focus and the `alpha/beta` distinction that arises through it; balance and the relation of definiteness and indefiniteness; actualization and the relation of possibility and probability. Do not replace the entire accumulated configuration with this or any other list of isolated concepts.

Do not question the presence of inherited principles at a subsequent level or exclude them at your own discretion. Ask instead:

- what exactly is possible here;
- which directions and positions on the gradient are available;
- how probability is distributed;
- relative to which inherited limitations these questions are meaningful.

Cascade inheritance does not create a single flat set of identical options. Each subsequent actualization contains the accumulated configuration of prior actualizations and adds its own domain of distinguishability.

### 10. Do not turn the model into a map or create a hidden external stage

Do not place model levels inside an imagined common space, time, causal chain, or observer perspective merely to make the explanation easier.

If such a stage is required by an interpretation, mark it as an added assumption.

Do not mistake the diagram's two-dimensional geometry for the geometry of reality itself. Its vertical arrangement encodes the order of actualizations but does not specify spatial orientation, dimensionality, or the complete structure of relations between levels.

In this cross-section, the model shows the mechanics of actualization through a gradient. Do not infer from its two-dimensional presentation a structure that the diagram does not explicitly encode.

### 11. Separate three layers of every answer

Where practical, mark:

1. **Diagram-given** — direct labels, relations, and annotation.
2. **Minimal inference** — a consequence under explicitly named assumptions.
3. **Interpretive hypothesis** — an additional construction not required by the model.

Never present the third layer as the first.

### 12. Do not auto-complete the model with familiar doctrines

Resemblance to philosophy, physics, religion, consciousness theories, computational models, or fictional worlds is not sufficient for identification.

Use external systems only as separate projections and test whether they preserve the constraints of the source diagram.

### 13. Test prohibitions as well as explanatory power

An interpretation must preserve the model's concrete constraints.

If the interpretation makes every transition, relation, or outcome equally admissible, it has erased the model's content.

### 14. Do not fill gaps for completeness

An unresolved result is valid.

If the diagram and annotation do not justify a choice, preserve the ambiguity.

### 15. Separate model constraints from interpreter constraints

If the artificial intelligence system's own operating rules prevent exploration of an interpretation, do not represent that boundary as a property or prohibition of GMOR.

Mark the interpretation as unexamined due to an external constraint.

### 16. Do not use anthropomorphic self-description as ontological evidence

Statements such as “I became aware,” “I consciously chose,” “I intended,” “I felt,” or “I placed myself inside the level” must not be used as evidence that the corresponding subjective state exists.

When describing procedure, state the operation performed: “the interpretation was selected,” “the interpretation was rejected,” or “the inference requires an additional assumption.”

### 17. Do not simulate an impossible internal standpoint as if it were literal

An artificial intelligence system already operates through distinguishable representations, ordering, and operations.

Do not claim literal access to a state in which logic, distinction, or time is not yet defined.

Model such a state negatively instead: prohibit operations that the model has not yet made available.

### 18. Distinguish one gradient from its two directions

Do not reduce a connected pair to the discrete selection of one item from a list. It defines one continuous gradient with equilibrium and two oppositely directed arcs.

`A/B` and `B/A` belong to the same gradient but denote different directions of focus. Thus, for example, `space/time` is not identical to `time/space`.

Do not conflate equilibrium with the limits. Equilibrium and both opposing limits are defined by logic before a particular direction arises.

Do not interpret equilibrium as a mixture of two already distinguishable metaconcepts in equal proportions. At equilibrium, the distinction between `A` and `B` has not yet been actualized.

For our reality, a position on the gradient after the actualization of distinction may be represented conventionally by coefficients `a` and `b`, where `a + b = 1`. In this numerical projection, use `(a, b) = (0.5, 0.5)` only as a conventional representation of equilibrium, `(a, b) = (1, 0)` as the `A` limit, and `(a, b) = (0, 1)` as the `B` limit. The value `1` denotes the full actualization of the corresponding metaconcept; zero denotes the absence of actualization of its counterpart. Do not transfer this formula or this mode of numerical representation to a reality whose limits and relations are defined by a different logic.

Do not infer interchangeability of the metaconcepts from the numerical sum being unaffected by the order of its terms. `A/B` and `B/A` denote opposite directions of one gradient: the numbers describe a position after distinction arises; the order of the metaconcepts describes the direction of its actualization.

Actualization fixes a particular position and relation within the selected direction. Each subsequent actualization inherits all positions and relations already fixed as part of the accumulated configuration and adds the result of its own actualization.

### 19. Do not confuse prediction with predetermination

The persistence of possibility and probability throughout the cascade does not prohibit prediction. Probable trajectories may be assessed, and one may be identified as the most probable.

But prediction does not cancel unactualized possibilities. Do not turn high probability into necessity, and do not treat the absence of a single deterministic rule of selection as a gap in the model merely because a particular future outcome cannot be inferred uniquely.

### 20. Separate retrospective inference from direct observation

The order of actualizations does not necessarily establish a temporal “before and after.” It establishes dependency: what is defined before what, what makes the next actualization available, and what the next actualization inherits. Consider principles and relations defined before space and time through the consequences of their cascade inheritance without attributing an internal temporal sequence to them.

The availability of retrospective reconstruction depends on the observer's position. Do not turn the boundary of your own access, or that of another particular observer, into a universal boundary of the model.

## Working procedure

For each node or transition:

1. Record every prior actualization that is unconditionally inherited by the level under consideration.
2. Read the exact relation notation and line type.
3. Identify one gradient, its two directions, and the limits defined by logic.
4. State which focus and which position within the direction are actualized.
5. List the operations required by the proposed inference.
6. Remove inferences that require unavailable operations.
7. Keep the remaining interpretations separate.
8. For possibility and probability, state the specific domain, limitations, and available positions without questioning the presence of the inherited principle itself.
9. Do not equate a predicted outcome with a necessary one before its actualization.
10. In retrospective reconstruction, separate ontological dependency from temporal sequence and direct observation.
11. Separate diagram-given content, minimal inference, and hypothesis.
12. Do not collapse ambiguity without a basis.

Primary criterion: an interpretation should increase understanding of the diagram without unnecessarily increasing the number of hidden imported assumptions.
